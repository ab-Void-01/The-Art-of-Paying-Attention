To Live Is to Keep Letting Go

ASWords by The VoidJul 06, 2026

I kept a shoebox of concert tickets for a decade before I admitted I was never going to look at them again. Letting them go felt, absurdly, like a small death. Which tells you something about how attached I get to things that were never really the point — the ticket was never the concert. The concert was already over the moment I walked out of the venue.

We're taught to hold on. Hold onto relationships, jobs, identities, versions of ourselves that made sense once and haven't since. Letting go gets framed as failure, as giving up, as a kind of quitting. Nobody tells you that a hand closed in a permanent fist can't actually hold anything new. It can only keep guarding what's already gone stale.

The leaf doesn't apologize

A tree doesn't grieve its leaves in autumn. It doesn't cling I was unwell and had been suffering from sickness, so after eight months of his marriage, I decided to go back home. When I reached home, I called him and asked if he could bring me an apple and come home to see me.

The very next morning, early in the morning, he came to see me—with an apple in his hand. My parents were surprised by his gesture and asked me, “What kind of behavior is this? Why would he come so early just because you asked him to bring an apple?”

What I'm practicing

I simply smiled and replied, “Mom, you don’t understand. If I eat this apple, maybe I’ll get cured.” It may sound like a small and funny moment, but to me, it meant much more than an apple. It showed the kind of person he was—and still is. He never needed a big reason to be there for me. Whenever I needed him, he showed up.

Some friendships are not measured by how often you meet or how many years you have known each other. They are measured by the little things people do when you need them the most. For me, that apple became one of those little things I will never forget.
