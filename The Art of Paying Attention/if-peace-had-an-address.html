If Peace Had an Address

ASWords by The VoidJul 12, 2026

If peace had an address, I don't think I'd move there. I think I'd visit, the way you visit a grandparent whose house always smells like something being baked and whose clock ticks louder than any clock has a right to. I'd stay for a weekend and come home lighter, and that lightness would last exactly as long as it takes to open my inbox.

I used to think peace was a place you eventually arrived at, like a destination you kept missing your flight to. Get the job, then peace. Pay off the debt, then peace. Finish the project, then, surely then. I have finished several projects now. Peace, it turns out, was never waiting at any of those gates.

A bench I keep thinking about

There's a bench by a lake I visited once, years ago, that I still think about more than most people I've actually stayed in touch with. It faced the water at an angle that caught the last light of the day, and someone had carved initials into the armrest so long ago they'd gone soft at the edges. I sat there for two hours doing absolutely nothing and it remains one of the most productive afternoons of my life.

Maybe peace isn't a place at all. Maybe it's just what's left when you finally stop applying for it.

The paperwork we do for stillness

It's strange how much effort we put into arriving somewhere that was never actually locked. I schedule my rest like a business meeting. I read articles about mindfulness while checking three other tabs. I have, on more than one occasion, rushed to relax. If peace has an address, I suspect I've driven past it a hundred times, going too fast to notice the mailbox.

So here's what I'm trying instead: fewer gates, more benches. Fewer conditions, more afternoons where the only agenda is the angle of the light. I can't promise I'll stay there. But I'm learning to visit more often, and to leave my shoes at the door when I do.

Wishing you a bench of your own,
The Void
