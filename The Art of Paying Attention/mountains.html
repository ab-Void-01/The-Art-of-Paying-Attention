A Letter to the Mountains I Haven't Met Yet

ASWords by The VoidJul 14, 2026

Dear mountains I haven't met yet — I think about you more than is reasonable for someone who has never stood in your shadow. I've memorized the shape of ranges from other people's photographs, traced ridgelines with a finger on a screen, and called it longing. Maybe it is. Maybe longing is just love that hasn't found its object yet.

There's a particular kind of quiet I imagine you keep — not the absence of sound but the sound of nothing trying to be heard. No notifications. No small talk. Just wind deciding where to go next, and grass that has stopped waiting for anyone's opinion of it. I think I could be a different, better version of tired there. The kind that comes from climbing instead of scrolling.

On the maps I haven't earned yet

I keep a list of you — half-remembered names from documentaries, a pass someone mentioned once at a dinner I barely paid attention to, a peak that shows up in the corner of a stranger's vacation photo. It isn't a bucket list. Bucket lists sound like things to finish. This is closer to a list of people I haven't been introduced to but already suspect I'll like.

The map is not the mountain, but it's the closest thing to a handshake I've managed so far.

I used to think the point of travel was arrival — the summit photo, the flag in the snow, proof for people who weren't there. Lately I think the point might be smaller and stranger than that: to become, for a few hours, someone the mountain has to take seriously. Not conquer it. Just be worth noticing by it.

What I hope you'll teach me

I hope you'll teach me patience, since you've clearly had millions of years of practice and still haven't rushed a single sunrise. I hope you'll teach me scale — how it feels to be correctly small, the good kind of small, the kind that isn't about being overlooked but about finally understanding where you stand. And selfishly, I hope you'll teach my legs that discomfort and defeat are not the same word.

Until then, I'll keep writing you letters you'll never read, practicing on smaller hills, saving up days off like coins in a jar labeled "someday, but soon." When I finally do meet you — whichever one of you decides to go first — I promise I'll arrive quietly, the way you'd want to be met. No fanfare. Just someone who's been paying attention from very far away, finally close enough to say hello properly.

Until the trailhead,
The Void
