I've Never Been There, Yet It Feels Like Home

ASWords by The VoidJul 10, 2026

There's a strange kind of homesickness for places you've never set foot in. Not nostalgia — you can't be nostalgic for something you haven't lived — but something adjacent to it, a longing shaped exactly like a memory even though the memory was never yours to have. I get it for four places in particular, and I don't fully understand why, which is probably the point.

Switzerland

I think it's the quiet precision of it — trains that arrive on the minute, lakes so still they look edited, mountains that seem to have been arranged rather than formed. I imagine myself there in autumn, saying very little, understood anyway.

Norway

Norway feels like the far end of a long exhale — fjords that don't perform for anyone, light that behaves strangely in summer, silence that isn't empty so much as full of something patient. I picture myself small there, and comfortable being so.

Turkey

Cappadocia looks like a place invented by someone with a stranger imagination than mine — valleys carved soft by centuries of wind, balloons drifting over them at dawn like something out of a dream I keep almost remembering. It feels ancient and unhurried at once.

Canada

Canada, to me, is scale — lakes the color of something mixed rather than found, forests that go on until the map gives up describing them. I imagine the cold there feeling less like weather and more like company.

Where the longing actually lives

None of this is really about geography. I don't think I'm longing for Switzerland's punctuality or Norway's fjords so much as for a version of myself who's unhurried enough to deserve them — patient the way those landscapes are patient, quiet the way they're quiet without needing to justify it.

Maybe home was never a place I hadn't found yet. Maybe it's a pace I haven't learned yet, and I keep mistaking it for somewhere on a map.

I'll get to all four eventually, I hope. But I suspect that when I do, what I'll actually be looking for isn't the lake or the fjord or the valley. It'll be the slower person I've been quietly practicing to become in the meantime.

Still packing, in my head,
The Void
