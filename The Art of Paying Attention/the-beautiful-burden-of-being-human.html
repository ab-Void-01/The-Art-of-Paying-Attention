The Beautiful Burden of Being Human

ASWords by The VoidJul 07, 2026

Nothing else we know of feels this much. As far as we can tell, no rock grieves, no river doubts itself, no mountain wonders whether it said the wrong thing at dinner. We got the rare and inconvenient gift of feeling every bit of it — the good, the humiliating, the unbearably tender — and no instruction manual came with the package.

I used to think of this as a design flaw. Why give a species enough imagination to dread its own death, enough memory to replay its worst mistakes at 3 a.m., enough love to make loss catastrophic? It seemed like an unnecessarily cruel joke, this business of being human.

The same door, both directions

But the door that lets in grief is the same door that lets in joy. You cannot dead-bolt one without losing the other. The capacity that makes heartbreak devastating is the exact same capacity that makes a good song devastating in the other direction — that makes a stranger's kindness, a child's laugh, an ordinary Tuesday sunset feel like more than the physics can account for.

We don't get to keep the wonder and return the weight. It's one ticket, and it covers the whole ride.

Carrying it well

I don't think the goal is to feel less. I've tried that route — numbness dressed up as maturity — and it costs more than it saves. The goal, I think, is to carry the weight without pretending it's light, and to keep noticing the wonder without needing it to cancel the weight out. Both are true at once. That's not a contradiction. That's apparently just what this is.

So: beautiful burden, then. Not despite the difficulty. Because of it. Because something that could feel nothing would also miss everything, and I would rather carry the whole heavy, luminous weight of it than trade the wonder away to travel light.

Carry it well, out there,
The Void
