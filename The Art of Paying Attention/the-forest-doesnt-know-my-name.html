The Forest Doesn't Know My Name

ASWords by The VoidJul 11, 2026

I like to walk into forests and announce nothing. No hiking app tracking my pace, no photo for later, just me and several thousand trees who could not care less that I've arrived. It's one of the few relationships in my life with absolutely no performance involved.

The forest doesn't know my name. It didn't clear its calendar for me. It was doing exactly this — being quietly, enormously itself — the day before I got here, and it will keep doing it long after I've gone back to a life full of people who do know my name and expect things from it.

The relief of being unrecognized

There's a particular kind of relief in that anonymity that I didn't expect the first time I felt it. Most of my life is spent being someone — an employee, a friend, a name on a form, a person who is expected to respond to messages within a reasonable window. The forest asks none of that. It doesn't care if I'm doing well. It just keeps growing, the way it was going to whether or not I showed up to watch.

Being unimportant to something enormous is, against all expectation, one of the more peaceful feelings available to a person.

What indifference teaches

I used to find that indifference lonely. Now I find it clarifying. Somewhere among the moss and the roots that have been quietly winning slow arguments with rock for longer than my family has had a surname, my problems shrink to their actual size. Not gone. Just correctly proportioned against something older than my worries and entirely uninterested in them.

I leave forests lighter every time, not because they gave me anything, but because they didn't take anything either. No name, no expectations, no record kept. Just the trees, doing what they do, letting me stand there and be, for an hour, wonderfully unnecessary.

Go be unnecessary somewhere green,
The Void
