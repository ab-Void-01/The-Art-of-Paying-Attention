The Person I Met After Getting Lost

ASWords by The VoidJul 13, 2026

I took the wrong turn on purpose, technically. I remember thinking the path looked more interesting than the one the map recommended, and I was right about that much — it was more interesting. It was also, within forty minutes, nowhere I recognized, with a phone at nine percent and a sky doing that thing where it stops deciding whether it's dusk.

I want to say I panicked gracefully. I did not. I stood at a fork in the trail arguing with myself in two voices, one annoyed and one frightened, neither of them useful. And that's when a woman came around the bend with a dog that clearly knew where it was going, which was more than either of us could say for me.

The kindness of strangers on trails

She didn't ask if I was lost. She just said, "This bit always confuses people," as though my confusion were a known local landmark rather than a personal failure. She walked me back to the junction I'd missed, and somewhere in that fifteen-minute walk she told me about her father, who'd taught her this trail when she was seven, and how she still came back every year around this time because grief, she said, likes a familiar path.

I never got her name. I didn't think to ask until she'd already turned off toward wherever she was going, dog trotting ahead like it had somewhere to be. I stood there for a second missing someone I'd known for a quarter of an hour.

Getting lost is just the world's slow way of introducing you to someone you'd have otherwise walked right past.

What the wrong turn was for

I think about her sometimes, usually when I'm annoyed at myself for some unplanned detour in ordinary life — a cancelled flight, a job that didn't work out, a relationship that ended somewhere I didn't recognize on the map I'd drawn for myself. I used to treat every wrong turn as a problem to be corrected as fast as possible. Now I wonder what I've walked past in my hurry to get back on route.

I made it back before dark, in the end, phone at two percent and pride fully intact. But the story I tell people isn't about the trail. It's about the stranger who found me exactly where I wasn't supposed to be, and gave me fifteen minutes of her father back in the process of getting me unlost.
