We Are All Visitors Here

ASWords by The VoidJul 08, 2026

I like airports for the same reason some people find them unbearable: nobody there is staying. Everyone is mid-journey, luggage in hand, headed toward a life that isn't happening in this terminal. It's honest, in a way most places aren't. It doesn't pretend to be permanent.

I think most places are airports and we just forget to notice. The apartment I've lived in for years, the job I show up to, even the body I'm writing this in — all of it borrowed, all of it temporary, all of it mine only for the length of the layover. We just don't get an intercom announcement reminding us of our gate.

The comfort in not staying

This used to unsettle me. Now, oddly, it's become one of the more comforting thoughts I carry around. If nothing here is mine to keep, then nothing here is mine to ruin permanently, either. The bad year doesn't own the whole story. The good year doesn't need to be defended against time, because time was always going to take its portion regardless.

We spend so much energy trying to make a layover feel like a residence. Maybe it was always meant to feel like a layover.

What visitors owe a place

I don't think being a visitor means being careless. If anything, it should mean the opposite — you treat a rented place more gently, sometimes, than one you assume you'll have forever. Maybe that's the posture worth practicing here: not grasping at permanence, but tending carefully to what's only ever passing through our hands.

Next time I'm in an airport, watching strangers hurry toward gates they'll forget the number of within a week, I'm going to try to remember that I'm one of them, everywhere, always. Not a resident of this life. A visitor, doing my best to leave the place a little better than the check-in desk found it.

Safe travels, fellow visitor,
The Void
