We Spend Our Lives Chasing Tomorrow

ASWords by The VoidJul 09, 2026

I have a folder on my phone labeled "someday." It has restaurant recommendations I haven't tried, a language app I opened once, a photo of a bookshelf design I meant to build. Someday is the most furnished room in my life and the one I visit least.

We are, most of us, professional tomorrow-chasers. We live for the weekend, the vacation, the promotion, the version of ourselves who will finally have time. And then the weekend arrives and we spend half of it recovering from the week and the other half preparing for the next one. Tomorrow keeps its distance on purpose, I think. It's better at running than we are at catching.

The math doesn't work

Here's the part that gets me: tomorrow, once it arrives, is just another today. It never announces itself as the destination. It shows up looking exactly like yesterday, full of its own unfinished errands, and we chase it forward again without ever noticing that we caught it and it wasn't what we were promised.

Somewhere in the chase, today became the thing we do while waiting for our life to start.

What I'm trying instead

I'm not arguing against planning — someday needs some tending, or it never comes at all. But I've started asking a smaller question before I defer something: is this actually waiting on a future condition, or am I just avoiding it? Half the things in my someday folder, it turns out, could have been today things. They were just wearing tomorrow's clothes to avoid the appointment.

I tried the restaurant last week. It wasn't life-changing. It was just dinner, on a Tuesday, in the actual present tense, which is apparently rarer than I'd like to admit. I'm working on more Tuesdays like that.

See you today, not someday,
The Void
